Architecture

One image, five stages, three models

A single detector produces confident-looking mistakes. This pipeline makes two independent detectors argue, fuses what they agree on, and then asks a dedicated classifier to confirm the species before anything is shown to you.

Contribution

Why this isn't just a YOLO wrapper

Wrapping a pretrained detector in an API is a solved problem. The work here is in what happens around the detector — a second opinion on every object, guard conditions on every refinement, and the willingness to return nothing at all.

01

Two detectors trained to disagree

The nano and small models differ by far more than capacity. They were trained from different seeds — 42 and 123 — on deliberately different augmentation recipes, with the small model adding mixup at 0.15 and copy-paste at 0.1. Decorrelated failure is the entire point: two models that make the same mistakes tell you nothing by agreeing. WBF then weights them [2, 1] rather than averaging them blindly.

02

A model allowed to say "no"

Two independent checks can stop the run: an unreadable image (blur variance below 5.0), or no single box reaching 0.40. Classification never executes, no boxes are drawn, and the response carries a warning naming the measured value that caused it. Returning nothing is treated as a correct outcome rather than a failure — which matters in underwater imaging, where a confident wrong species is far more damaging than an honest refusal. Keeping the two checks separate matters as much as having them: judging on an average let a crowd of weak detections outvote strong ones, and let an unreadable frame pass whenever the detector was confidently wrong about noise.

03

Refinement that has to prove itself

The zoom-and-recheck pass may only replace a box when the re-detected region overlaps the original at IoU > 0.4 and scores strictly higher. Both guards must hold or the original survives untouched. A second look is permitted to improve an answer, never to invent one — so the refinement step cannot drift onto a different object.

04

Three opinions, not two

When the detector and classifier disagree, the system doesn't pick a favourite. It manufactures a third opinion by re-classifying a looser 15% crop, then takes a majority vote. Two matching opinions win; a genuine three-way deadlock defaults to the detector and forfeits the agreement boost. Either way the disagreement is surfaced in the result rather than quietly resolved.

None of this requires a larger model or more data. It is the same 638-image dataset most aquarium-detection projects use — the difference is entirely in the decision logic wrapped around it, and every one of those decisions is visible in the output.

    Why keep the weaker model?

    The nano detector is not redundant. It was trained with a different seed and a different augmentation recipe, so it fails on different images than the small variant. Weighted Boxes Fusion exploits exactly that — it weights nano at 2 against small at 1 because agreement between two dissimilar models is stronger evidence than a single confident score.
